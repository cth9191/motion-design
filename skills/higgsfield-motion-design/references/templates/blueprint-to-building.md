## Asset plan

Apply these asset suggestions only when identity accuracy or the selected model requires them. Prompt-only is valid when sufficient; prefer existing assets and add generated references only for a specific need. Distinct shot compositions do not require an image or video job per shot.

Obtain matching plans, elevations and renders for a real building. Check floor count, openings and signature geometry before animation. For a concept, design one consistent model and derive both drawing and built views from it. A disconnected blueprint image and unrelated building image cannot establish accurate continuity. Exact proof lines may require 3D rendering/compositing instead of a generative morph.

## Default design bindings

Blueprint #16233F, drawing/type #EAF2FF, accent #D1EF17, faint grid rgba(234,242,255,0.12); default concept materials: concrete #CFC8BC, oak #B08954, planting #5E8C5A. Use crisp thin CAD linework without glow. The accent stays green-biased under golden light. A real project’s supplied materials take priority.

## Audio branch

The source prompt is silent. For a new launch/motion campaign, replace its silence clauses with instrumental music and synchronized effects and record that departure. Preserve an explicit silent request. Do not add narration by default.

## Parameterized mapping aid — adapt the original text above

```text
{{FORMAT}} | {{BRAND_OR_SUBJECT}} | {{AUDIO_SPEC}}

WORLD, SUBJECT AND DESIGN
Create one continuous 15-second architectural journey for {{DEVELOPMENT}}. The drawing and built structure share {{GEOMETRY_REFERENCE}}, including each floor, opening and terrace. Begin in dark blueprint blue with fine white CAD lines, a faint measured grid, hatching and arrowed dimensions. {{SIGNATURE_LINE}} is an existing distinctive rail, edge or structural line, colored in the brand accent in both drawing and building. Use {{MATERIAL_PALETTE}} for the actual architecture. The accent remains visually consistent under warm light. Clearly identify invented developments as concepts and do not add real availability claims.

CAMERA, EDITING AND MOTION
Keep one wide lens, approximately 84-degree diagonal field of view, for the entire take. The reference uses noticeable anamorphic edge character; protect readable graphics inside the central seventy percent. Move from face-on drafting plane to rising clockwise orbit, overhead descent into the plan, eye-level interior glide, terrace passage, then backward ascent. Join these moves continuously with smooth velocity. Transitions happen through the geometry and linework; no screen wipe or split-view boundary. Once materialized, the exterior stays built until the final un-plot.

TIMELINE — default 15-second adaptation; retime explicitly for other delivery lengths.

[0.00–1.80s] Phase 1 — plotted section
Start on empty blueprint paper. Plot an accent centerline, then the actual section around it: floor plates, setbacks and circulation. Add small dimension arrows and a project revision stamp. Draw {{HEADLINE_1}} stroke by stroke. Rotate the complete drawing plane into space at the end while keeping the lines visible.

[1.80–3.60s] Phase 2 — wireframe volume
Develop the section into the full axonometric building, stacking the correct number of floors in order. Continue into a rightward orbit. Attach {{VERIFIED_FEATURE_LABELS}} to the appropriate features. Geometry is still drawing, with no premature photoreal surfaces.

[3.60–5.80s] Phase 3 — line-to-material ignition
Start at ground level. {{SIGNATURE_LINE}} becomes material along its own path, then neighboring floor edges, glazing frames and landscape details acquire volume through connected strokes. Advance floor by floor as the camera rises with it. Above that advancing level retain wireframe. Let the surrounding paper develop into sky outward from the building silhouette. Show {{HEADLINE_3}}. Never replace this with a sweeping screen mask.

[5.80–7.80s] Phase 4 — plan descent and interior
Crest the orbit above a terrace, tilt to a straight-down apartment plan, and descend. The plan’s lines extrude past the camera into walls, flooring and furniture at their drawn positions. Keep {{PLAN_AREA}} only if verified. Settle into an eye-level glide toward the full-height glazing. The new interior must fit the exterior geometry.

[7.80–9.60s] Phase 5 — terrace passage
Continue through the glazing onto the actual terrace, with no cut or distortion spike. Reveal the signature edge, planting and neighboring setbacks. Plot {{TERRACE_LABELS}} on leaders tied to those features. Begin a smooth backward move that continues through the next phases.

[9.60–11.40s] Phase 6 — geometry proof
While pulling back, draw blueprint lines directly on their matching built edges: floors, mullions and signature rails. Hold drawing and building together long enough to demonstrate agreement. Sink the lines into those surfaces without changing geometry. Show {{HEADLINE_6}}. Begin a continuous lighting shift toward golden hour.

[11.40–13.20s] Phase 7 — wide architectural result
Continue backward and upward to reveal the complete building in warm light. Keep a faint blueprint grid only in the sky. Place {{RELEASE_COPY}} in small title-block chips only when supplied or verified; omit unsupported dates, prices and scarcity. Maintain the accent hue and let planting move subtly.

[13.20–14.60s] Phase 8 — drawing lockup and hold
Use 13.2–13.6 seconds to finish the pullback and gather the remaining lines into an underline for {{DEVELOPMENT}}. Add {{BRAND_LINE}} and {{CTA}} in architectural lettering. Stop the camera by 13.6 and hold for one second through 14.6.

[14.60–15.00s] Phase 9 — return to blueprint
Retract the drawing and built composition through a deliberately designed reverse-plot finish, returning to the empty blueprint field by 14.9. Leave the last 0.1 seconds clean. This is the preset’s in-world ending, not a generic opacity fade.

COPY AND REFERENCES
```

## Fidelity checks

- No cuts, wipes or mismatched buildings; drawing lines land on their actual material counterparts.
- Materialization advances through connected geometry from low floors upward.
- The route includes wireframe, materialization, plan/interior, terrace and proof overlay.
- The one-second lockup and reverse-plot return fit the 15-second timeline.
- All property dimensions and sales details are supported or explicitly conceptual.

Compare these checks with the rendered motion, not just the prompt. A missing signature action is a failed shot even if its materials look polished. Repair that shot or report the specific departure; do not relabel a generic result as this preset.
